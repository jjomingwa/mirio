# Premium 2D Action-Platformer Market Research

- Checked: 2026-07-28
- Market: Steam, United States base-price reference
- Scope: indie 2D platformer, action-platformer, metroidvania, and close premium comparables with a USD 20+ list price

## Reading Rules

- `List price` means the undiscounted US base price shown by Steam. It is not proof that most customers paid that amount.
- Review counts are snapshots, not unit sales. Steam may show English-only or all-language counts depending on locale and account settings.
- Store descriptions are evidence of what each product promises customers, not independent proof that every promised feature is equally good.
- Public conversion benchmarks use different denominators. Never compare two percentages until denominator, time window, price, acquisition source, and refund treatment match.

## Evidence: What the Comparable Pages Show

1. The sampled base-price band is $24.99-$29.99, with one adjacent lower-bound example at $21.99 excluded from the final nine-title table.
2. The strongest visible review signals cluster around 90%-96% positive with thousands or tens of thousands of reviews.
3. Each highly rated product has a short, legible product hook:
   - deflection combat;
   - responsive procedural combat;
   - defeated bosses becoming skills;
   - systemic multi-use puzzle tools;
   - inherited classes and progression.
4. Successful pages combine the hook with several value supports: nonlinear exploration, bosses, secrets, build variety, replayability, story/lore, or long-tail discoveries.
5. Achievements, Steam Cloud, controller-oriented play, and multi-language support are common baseline expectations rather than sufficient differentiators.
6. Strong visual and audio identity is repeatedly presented as part of the product, not as decoration added after mechanics.
7. GRIME, Dead Cells, and Blasphemous 2 show that post-launch expansions, DLC, or editions can extend value, but these are not substitutes for a strong launch build.

## Evidence: Valve Guidance and Measurement Tools

The following are official Valve/Steamworks sources. Wording here is paraphrased.

### Pricing

- Official link: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/pricing
- Valve recommends considering play length, replayability, multiplayer, Early Access state, comparable products, and future promotions.
- The objective is not simply to undercut competitors; the product must deliver strong value for the chosen price.
- Valve generally suggests an optional launch discount around 10%-15%.
- Practical consequence: a $24.99 base price with a 10%-15% launch discount produces a launch transaction price of about $22.49-$21.24, remaining above USD 20.

### User Reviews

- Official link: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/reviews
- Review scores summarize recent and lifetime recommendation sentiment.
- Valve describes reviews as a way to evaluate whether the expectations created by trailers, screenshots, marketing, and previews match the delivered experience.
- Reviews should be used as feedback evidence; they must not be manipulated or rewarded.

### Wishlists

- Official link: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/wishlist
- Wishlists represent interest generated by awareness and marketing.
- Steam can notify wishlisters at launch, on qualifying discounts, and once when an associated demo becomes available.
- The detailed wishlist report tracks additions, purchases, and deletions.
- Valve explicitly states that there is no accurate universal formula that converts wishlist count into predicted sales because users wishlist for different reasons.

### Visibility

- Official link: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/visibility
- Purchases and play are important Steam interest signals.
- Supported languages and accurate tags affect matching and visibility.
- Store traffic alone and store-page conversion rate alone are not direct visibility-ranking factors.
- A generic conversion target must therefore not be treated as an algorithm exploit.

### Store and Platform Traffic

- Official link: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/traffic_reporting
- Steam reports impressions, visits, click-through rate where measurable, and traffic sources.
- An impression only means the capsule or name was displayed; it does not prove that a person consciously saw it.
- A visit is a product-page load and can occur more than once before purchase.

### UTM Analytics

- Official link: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/utm_analytics
- Steam reports total, trusted, and logged-in tracked visits for tagged links.
- Supported conversion events include wishlist additions and purchases.
- A purchase or wishlist is attributed when it occurs within 72 hours of the tracked visit.
- Conversion data is finalized about four days after the visit.
- Cross-browser, cross-device, and some in-app-browser journeys cannot always be connected while preserving user privacy.

### Refund Reporting

- Official link: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/finance/refunds
- Steamworks exposes recent and lifetime refund data and player-provided refund reasons.
- Refund evidence should be used to find bugs, balance failures, and expectation mismatches.
- UTM purchase conversion and refund reporting are separate reports; do not claim campaign-level retained purchase attribution unless the available data genuinely supports that join.

### Steam Playtest

- Official link: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/playtest
- Steam Playtest uses a separate child App ID and allows limited or open cohorts without contaminating the base game's reviews or wishlist count.
- Developers control cohort size and when the test becomes playable.
- Steam does not collect structured design feedback automatically; the game should provide a clear feedback route.
- New tester cohorts can be used to prevent experienced testers from hiding onboarding problems.

### Demos

- Official link: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/application/demos
- A demo is normally a small but representative slice that demonstrates the core mechanics and leaves demand for more.
- Because customers use it for purchase decisions, Valve advises releasing a high-quality demo that reflects the full experience.
- Valve advises balancing enough content to create confidence against giving away the whole experience.
- The demo can link to the full product through the Steam Overlay, and demo/full-game saves can share Steam Cloud where appropriate.

### Steam Next Fest

- Official link: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest
- Next Fest promotes upcoming games with public demos and supports feedback gathering.
- A game may participate in only one Next Fest.
- Valve's build review checks basic operation and promised features; it is not a complete QA pass.
- Therefore the representative demo and its telemetry should pass internal gates before spending the one-time Next Fest opportunity.

## Evidence: List Price Is Not Transaction Price

- On the check date, Nine Sols showed a $29.99 base price and a $14.99 promotion.
- On the check date, Aeterna Noctis showed a $29.99 base price and a $5.99 promotion.
- Other comparables also use recurring discounts and bundles.
- Lifetime reviews therefore cannot establish that reviewers paid USD 20 or more.
- Any claim about performance at USD 20+ must filter or design the experiment around the actual checkout price, not the store's crossed-out base price.

## Evidence: External Conversion Benchmarks Are Directional Only

### GameDiscoverCo 2024-2025 summary

- Source: https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gamediscoverco-the-state-of-steam
- Reported median first-week `total units / launch wishlists` was about 0.15 for games with more than 25,000 launch wishlists and about 0.10 for titles priced above $10.
- This numerator includes purchases by people who were not wishlisters. It is a sales-to-wishlist ratio, not a true person-level wishlist cohort conversion rate.
- Performance varied by an order of magnitude, so it is unsuitable as a universal pass/fail formula.

### Immutable 2026 vendor benchmark

- Source: https://www.immutable.com/insights/steam-wishlist-conversion-rates
- The vendor reports a 5%-10% baseline and 15%-25% for activated audiences.
- The methodology is aggregated from publisher conversations, platform analytics, and the vendor's customers rather than an independent public dataset.
- Use it only as directional evidence that a generic 50% wishlist-to-purchase goal is far above commonly reported ranges.

## Why a Generic "50% Purchase Rate" Is Undefined

The phrase is missing a denominator. Each of the following is a different metric:

1. visible impression to purchase;
2. Steam product-page visit to purchase;
3. UTM tracked visit to purchase;
4. wishlist at launch to purchase;
5. demo activation to purchase;
6. demo first launch to purchase;
7. demo completion to purchase;
8. completed-demo purchase-CTA click to purchase;
9. checkout start to completed payment.

It is also missing:

- attribution window;
- list price versus actual paid price;
- launch discount and regional price;
- acquisition channel;
- new prospects versus existing fans;
- owners and duplicate visitors;
- refund treatment;
- sample size and uncertainty.

No agent may report "50% achieved" until all of those fields are fixed and the authoritative report has been inspected.

## Chosen Qualified-Cohort Definition

The retained stretch KPI is intentionally narrow and high-intent:

> Among logged-in Steam users who complete the representative demo and use its end-of-demo purchase CTA to create a tracked Steam store visit, the share that purchases within Steam's 72-hour UTM attribution window at an actual checkout price of at least USD 20.

### Funnel

`demo first launch -> representative slice complete -> purchase CTA -> Steam UTM tracked visit -> paid within 72 hours`

### Required implementation

- Use a build-specific link such as:
  - `utm_source=demo`
  - `utm_medium=game_overlay`
  - `utm_campaign=build_X`
  - `utm_content=completed`
- Keep the end-of-demo CTA wording and placement stable while comparing gameplay builds.
- Keep base price, launch discount, store assets, campaign source, territory, and observation window stable when comparing rates.
- Measure demo first launches and completions with privacy-conscious aggregate telemetry.
- Use Steam's UTM report as the purchase authority for tracked visits and 72-hour purchases.

### Formula

`qualified_purchase_rate = UTM purchases / UTM tracked visits from the completed-demo CTA`

This is a `tracked-visit-to-purchase` metric conditioned on reaching the completed-demo CTA. It is not the percentage of all people who saw, downloaded, or started the game.

### Statistical proof gate

- Minimum denominator: 400 qualified UTM tracked visits.
- Minimum numerator: 220 attributed purchases.
- Observed rate: `220 / 400 = 0.55`.
- Approximate two-sided 95% Wilson lower confidence bound: 0.50.
- Do not stop at a point estimate of exactly 50%; with finite data its confidence interval extends below 50%.

### Refund audit

- Wait for the relevant refund observation period and inspect Steamworks Refund Reporting.
- Report gross UTM conversion and refund evidence separately.
- Do not subtract an assumed refund percentage or imply an exact campaign-level retained rate unless Steam's available reports support that attribution.
- High refund incidence or repeated expectation-mismatch reasons fail the commercial-quality gate even if gross UTM conversion passes.

## Inference: Product and Release Gates

The following are recommendations derived from the evidence, not facts guaranteed by Steam.

1. Use $24.99 as the default premium-price hypothesis. With Valve's suggested 10%-15% launch discount, the transaction price stays above $20.
2. Treat $29.99 as a higher bar that requires especially strong audiovisual identity, content depth, and external proof.
3. Target at least 90% positive paid-review sentiment after meaningful sample sizes. This is a quality objective derived from successful comparables, not a Steam ranking rule.
4. Require one clearly communicable mechanical hook that appears in capsule/trailer messaging and is felt in the first playable session.
5. Do not count feature quantity, achievement count, or game length as proof of fun. Aeterna Noctis is the cautionary example.
6. Validate controls, readability, onboarding, difficulty fairness, audiovisual feedback, save integrity, gamepad behavior, and performance before expanding content.
7. Run Steam Playtest in fresh cohorts before publishing the permanent public demo.
8. Spend the one eligible Next Fest only after the representative demo passes completion, feedback, technical, and qualified-conversion gates.
9. Track the full funnel separately:
   - impression to visit;
   - visit to wishlist;
   - demo launch to completion;
   - completion to CTA;
   - CTA tracked visit to purchase;
   - wishlist cohort purchases at 7 and 30 days;
   - refunds and refund reasons;
   - paid-review score and recurring complaints.
10. A 50% qualified CTA-visit conversion does not imply mass-market 50% purchase intent. Commercial success still requires enough qualified traffic and enough retained buyers.

## Low-Intelligence Agent Guardrail

Before claiming commercial progress, the agent must write down:

1. exact build identifier;
2. exact customer cohort;
3. exact denominator and numerator;
4. country/region scope;
5. actual checkout price and discount;
6. attribution window;
7. traffic source and UTM tags;
8. sample size and confidence interval;
9. refund report status;
10. authoritative report links or exported evidence.

If any item is missing, the result is `UNVERIFIED`, not a pass.
